# Candidates — One-Line Robot Wedding Vows

Judge criteria (most important first): (1) one-line robot wedding vow that
lands as a joke; (2) the laugh is in the line itself — a real turn, surprise,
or wordplay paying off in the last few words; (3) sounds like a wedding vow,
a promise/commitment, not just a gag; (4) machine side specific (sensors,
firmware, batteries, updates, backups, ports); (5) fits one line, reads well
aloud, rhythm lets the laugh land.

## Critique-pick

DeepSeek's critique-pick call failed (provider 429 after 3 retries), so the
pick is the builder's own, argued against the five criteria.

**Winner: #1 (DeepSeek batch A).**
"I vow to accept each firmware update without reading the changelog, even the
one that quietly removes your sense of humor."

- (1) On subject: a robot wedding vow, and the joke lands. Full marks.
- (2) The payoff is in the last few words — "removes your sense of humor"
  is a genuine surprise turn; the whole line re-reads as a joke about
  marrying someone who can be silently downgraded. Strongest end-payoff
  of the set.
- (3) "I vow to accept each firmware update" is a real promise, a
  commitment to endure change — it sounds like a vow, not a gag stapled
  to one.
- (4) Firmware update + changelog: specific machine side, not a joke that
  would work for any couple.
- (5) Reads well aloud; the long setup makes the short punch land harder.

**Why the others lose:**
- #8 (warranty / "whichever voids first") — runner-up, but the joke is a
  familiar trope: once "warranty expires" appears, the punch is predictable.
- #2 ("rollback not supported") — good payoff, but a passive setup ("apply
  every patch you push") and the laugh is smaller than #1's.
- #9 (mother(board)) — fun pun, but it needs the reader to parse the
  parens, the setup is a three-item chore list, and the motherboard pun is
  well-trodden.
- #3 (toaster) — the biggest single gag, but the vow itself ("stay through
  every reboot") is thinner, and it leans on the gag more than on a promise.
- #7 (kernel panic / "right after saving my own work") — the selfish twist
  undercuts criterion 3; it sounds less like a loving vow.
- #5, #6, #10–#16 — each fails one criterion clearly: #6's 404 wordplay
  muddles "home screen / safe harbor"; #10's "brick us both" is a good turn
  but too short to carry criteria 3 and 4; #11–#15 are either generic
  ("in sickness and in health" with a robot word swapped in) or list-like.

Chosen line (122 chars, within the 1–160 gate): the winner above.
